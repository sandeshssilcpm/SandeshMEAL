How to read: these cards add up whatever is currently filtered. £ per person = total £ cost ÷ people served. People per tap = people served ÷ tap stands (lower means less crowding at each tap).

Year-wise

How to read: each bar is one fiscal year (e.g. 2015/16). Bar height = the measure picked in the dropdown (projects built, people served, tap stands, pipe km or cost). Use it to see when work was busiest. Click a bar to filter the whole dashboard to that year; click again to clear. Population, households and tap stands were not recorded before about 1988/89, so early years look empty for those measures.

AWC-wise

How to read: each bar is one AWC. A longer bar means that AWC has more of the chosen measure. The second dropdown changes which AWC column is used for grouping. Click a bar to filter everything to that AWC.

District-wise

How to read: each bar is one district, ranked from highest to lowest for the chosen measure. Use the number dropdown to show the top 10, 15, 25, 40 or all districts. Click a bar to filter the dashboard to that district.

Province

How to read: the ring shows each province’s share of the chosen measure (percent shown in the legend). A bigger slice means a bigger share. Hover a slice for the exact number.

Phase-wise

How to read: each bar is one project phase (Phase I–V, RWEPP, CEDAR, GWT). It shows how much was delivered in each phase. Click a bar to filter to that phase.

Key insights

    How to read: these lines are written automatically from the data and change when you apply filters.

    Funder over the years

    How to read: each year’s bar is split by funder colour, so you can see who funded the work in each period (for example CEDAR in the early years, then DFID, GWT and FCDO). Change the dropdown to compare projects, people or cost by funder.

    Cumulative reach

    How to read: each line adds up year by year, so it only goes up. A steeper climb means faster growth in people, households or tap stands reached. The right end of each line is the total to date.

    Cost per person served (£) by year

    How to read: for each year, total £ cost ÷ total people served (only projects where population was recorded). A rising line means water costs more per person, which can reflect longer pipelines, harder terrain or higher prices.

    People per tap stand by year

    How to read: for each year, people served ÷ tap stands. A higher point means more people share each tap; a lower point means better access per person.

    Correlation explorer vs

    How to read: each dot is one project; pick what goes on the X and Y axes in the dropdowns. The dashed line is the overall trend. r runs from −1 to +1: close to +1 means the two measures rise together, near 0 means no real link, negative means one rises as the other falls. Tick log scale to spread out the dots, because a few very large projects otherwise squash the rest. Dot colour = funder; hover a dot to see the project.

    Correlation matrix (Pearson r)

    How to read: each square compares two measures and shows their correlation r. Blue = they rise together, red = opposite direction. A darker square means a stronger link and the number is the exact r. The diagonal is always 1.00 because a measure matches itself. Only projects where both values are recorded (>0) are used.

    AWC × 5-year period (uses AWC metric)

    How to read: each row is an AWC and each column is a 5-year period (for example 15–19 = fiscal years 2015 to 2019). A darker square means more of the chosen measure in that period. It shows when each AWC was most active. The measure follows the AWC chart’s dropdown.

    £ per person by AWC

    How to read: total £ cost ÷ people served for each AWC. A longer red bar means each person served cost more there. Compare with the pipe-length correlation to see if long pipelines explain the higher cost.

    Project records

    How to read: every project is one row. Click a column heading to sort. Edit changes a row and Delete removes it. The search box filters the whole dashboard, and all columns shows all 27 headings.
